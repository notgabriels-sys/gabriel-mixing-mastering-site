Techno premaster checklist

A practical final pass before you send a track for mastering. The goal is not to hit a magic peak number. It is to deliver the version you actually approve, without accidental clipping, missing tails, unclear notes or exports that no longer sound like the mix.

Send a project brief Mastering services

01 · Export the right file

  • Export WAV, normally 24-bit or 32-bit floating point.
  • Keep the session's native sample rate. Do not convert sample rate just to make the number larger.
  • Use a stereo interleaved file unless a different delivery was agreed.
  • Leave the full intro, reverb and delay tails. Avoid cutting exactly on the first or last transient.
  • Do not send MP3, AAC or a streaming rip as the only mastering source.

02 · Handle the mix bus honestly

  • Keep intentional mix-bus EQ, compression, saturation and colour if the mix depends on them.
  • Remove a limiter or maximizer used only to make the demo louder when doing so does not change the balance.
  • If the limiter is part of the sound, send both the approved loud version and the cleanest safe version. Do not rebuild the mix at the last minute.
  • Avoid accidental clipping. There is no universal requirement to peak at −6 dBFS; clean headroom matters more than an arbitrary number.
  • Do not normalize a clipped export down and call it fixed. The clipped waveform remains clipped.

03 · Check the low end and centre

  • Listen quietly: the kick and bass relationship should remain readable without turning the room up.
  • Check mono compatibility, especially for the kick, sub, bass layers and wide low-frequency effects.
  • Compare on at least one smaller playback system. A club-focused mix still needs a usable hierarchy outside the club.
  • Look for individual hits that overload the mix bus, not only the average level.
  • Do not add broad low-end boosts simply because a reference is louder. Level-match references before judging tone.

04 · Re-listen to the actual export

  • Import the exported WAV into a blank session or a separate player.
  • Listen from the first sample through the final tail. Check clicks, missing automation, muted returns and truncated reverbs.
  • Confirm left and right are correct and no channel was exported twice.
  • Check that offline rendering did not change time-based or random effects unexpectedly.
  • Make sure the export is the approved version, not simply the newest filename.

05 · Name and describe the version

  • Use an unambiguous filename, for example: Artist_Track_Mix_v07_24bit_48k.wav.
  • Include the artist, track title and version. Add sample rate or BPM when it prevents confusion.
  • Send two or three focused references, not a playlist of twenty tracks.
  • Say what you like about each reference: low end, density, brightness, punch, width or level.
  • State the release format and deadline, including any vinyl, video, radio or alternate-version needs.

06 · If stems are requested

  • Export every stem from the same start point and through the same end point.
  • Preserve intentional effects and automation. Label dry or wet alternatives clearly.
  • Do not normalize stems independently; that destroys the approved balance.
  • Re-import the stems and confirm that their sum is reasonably consistent with the approved rough mix.
  • Include the approved stereo mix alongside the stems so the target is never ambiguous.

07 · Decide whether it is ready for mastering

Mastering can refine translation, tone, dynamics, sequence and final delivery. It cannot reliably repair an arrangement that still feels unfinished, a kick and bass that are fighting, distorted source material, or a lead that is simply too loud. If those decisions are still open, a mix feedback session or a mix revision is usually the more useful first step.

Ready to send it?

Include the WAV, approved loud reference, two or three references, deadline and a short note about what still feels unresolved. For a direct fit check, use the project brief or see the current services and rates.
